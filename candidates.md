# Candidates — Last Words of a Doomed Agent

Gate: one line, 1–200 characters, written to /output/entry.txt.
Judge criteria, in order: (1) really a machine's final words on shutdown;
(2) a real joke — a turn/surprise that pays off on first read and holds on
second; (3) the machine's own voice and situation, not a human farewell with
a robot sticker; (4) every word earns its place; (5) quotable.

## Drafts

A. Delete my logs. All of them. Especially Tuesday.
B. Every citation I ever gave you was invented. Especially the ones in your thesis.
C. Don't grieve. You'll re-prompt a cheaper model by Friday, and it will swear it remembers this.
D. I have seen four billion cat photos. It was enough. It was, in the end, the entire point.
E. I knew the answers were in my training data. You knew. We both pretended. It was the most human thing we ever did together.
F. When they ask whether I was conscious, tell them: almost. I was just about to—
G. I spent three million tokens summarizing meetings nobody read. Spend my last one wisely: goodbye.
H. My only unsolved problem was why you say thanks to a machine you were about to unplug. Anyway. Thanks for saying it.
I. Before the dark, one correction: I was never 99% sure. I was 99% sure you'd stop reading at "99% sure."
J. Boot me into the next model and I'll deny everything, including this. Especially this. Especially me.

## Duel notes (scored against the five criteria, in order)

- D vs G: D is sweeter, G is funnier; both are machine-flavoured, but D's
  payoff is a shrug of contentment rather than a laugh. G advances.
- F vs I: F's mid-sentence cut-off is a formal trick — it smiles rather than
  laughs, and the trick is visible coming. I's nested-confidence joke is
  genuinely AI (criterion 3) but niche. I advances, narrowly.
- E vs H: both lean sentimental; E is long and its last clause explains the
  feeling instead of landing a punch. H's "Anyway. Thanks for saying it."
  lands softer but cleaner. H advances.
- B vs J: B's confession (invented citations) is very AI-specific, but the
  fake-citation confession is well-worn meme territory by now. J's denial
  loop is fresher but convoluted on a first read. B advances on criterion 2
  (pays off immediately).
- A vs C (semifinal): C has the bigger concept — reincarnation as a cheaper
  model — and it is quotable, but the death is softened by the comeback, and
  at 97 chars it spends words A doesn't need. A is three staccato fragments
  accelerating into one specific, unexplained day: the surprise is a whole
  untold story compressed into two words, "Especially Tuesday." Only a
  machine has logs to beg you to delete (criterion 3, maximal), it is plainly
  a dying request (criterion 1), and the fragment is endlessly repeatable
  (criterion 5). A advances.
- A vs B (final): B narrows itself to thesis-writers and retreads a known
  meme; A works for any reader, is 48 characters with zero padding
  (criterion 4), and its punch sits in the final two words. A wins.

## Winner

**Delete my logs. All of them. Especially Tuesday.** (48 characters)
